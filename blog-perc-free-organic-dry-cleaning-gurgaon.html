The Dark Side of PERC (Perchloroethylene)

Perchloroethylene, commonly known as PERC, was introduced in the 1930s as a heavy-duty industrial degreaser. Because it dissolves grease quickly and cheaply, it became the global standard for dry cleaning. However, modern environmental and medical science paints a starkly different picture:

  • Probable Human Carcinogen: The World Health Organization’s International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) and the US EPA classify PERC as a toxic air contaminant and Group 2A probable human carcinogen.
  • Severe Skin & Respiratory Irritation: Residual PERC molecules trapped in dry-cleaned wool blazers, winter sweaters, or bedsheets off-gas directly against human skin, causing contact dermatitis, eczema flare-ups, and nighttime wheezing in asthmatic children.
  • Fabric Degradation Over Time: Chlorinated solvents aggressively strip the natural lanolin oils from pure wool and dry out silk filaments, causing fine suits to become brittle, lose drape, and fray along lapel seams.

The Gurgaon Hard Water Crisis: Why Your White Shirts Turn Yellow

Beyond dry cleaning solvents, daily water-wash laundry in Gurgaon faces another notorious obstacle: groundwater hardness. Borewell water across sectors along Sohna Road, Dwarka Expressway, and New Gurgaon regularly registers Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) levels between 1,200 to 2,500 ppm, saturated with calcium, magnesium, and dissolved iron.

💧 The RO Water Softening Difference: When hard water mixes with commercial laundry detergent, minerals bind with soap molecules to form insoluble soap curd (calcium stearate). This gray-yellow film deposits into cotton fibers, turning crisp white office shirts dull and yellow after just 3 washes. Mourya processes all wash & fold and wash & iron orders using soft, RO-filtered water (TDS < 150 ppm), guaranteeing that white garments stay dazzling white and colors remain brilliantly vivid wash after wash.

Frequently Asked Questions About Organic Fabric Care

Why do clothes from local dry cleaners smell like petrol or chemicals?
Local neighborhood dry cleaners and cheap dhobis frequently use raw kerosene, industrial petrol, or recycled Perchloroethylene (PERC) without adequate distillation systems. This leaves volatile chemical residues trapped in the garment's fibers, emitting that familiar, toxic chemical stench.
What is PERC and why is it dangerous for your family?
Perchloroethylene (PERC) is a synthetic chlorinated hydrocarbon classified as a toxic environmental pollutant and probable human carcinogen. Residues remaining in infant wear, sweaters, or bedsheets can trigger contact dermatitis, asthma flare-ups, and respiratory irritation.
What makes Mourya’s dry cleaning 100% organic and skin-safe?
Mourya uses advanced isoparaffinic hydrocarbon solvents imported from Europe that are non-chlorinated, biodegradable, completely odorless, and dermatologically tested safe for sensitive skin, allergy sufferers, and infant wear.
Does organic hydrocarbon dry cleaning clean as effectively as PERC?
Even better! While PERC is harsh and strips natural fibers of their luster and elasticity, organic hydrocarbon solvents dissolve oils, greases, and make-up while preserving fabric softness, color vibrancy, and delicate embellishments.
